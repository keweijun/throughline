# Using the learning interface

Open Throughline from its launcher, or ask Codex to open a course (`guided-learning open <course>`); the interface runs in the background until `guided-learning stop`. It opens on **Your courses**, the course library; **Courses** in the top bar returns to it. For a course outside the library, run `guided-learning-web --workspace path/to/course` and keep the terminal open; Ctrl+C stops it. Choose English (default) or Simplified Chinese under Language in **Settings** (the gear in the top bar), which also holds Appearance (light, dark or matching the system) and the Teacher settings. The selection is course-owned and survives browser reloads and host restarts. It updates interface text without discarding input drafts, while sources and saved records keep their original language.

New lessons, side answers, supplements, exercises, feedback and preparation use the selected teaching language. Running requests finish in their original language; regenerating an incomplete reply uses the current selection and retains the earlier attempt. Changing language invalidates previous-language preparation without changing the teaching position or mastery evidence.

## Learning

- **Route** (left): sections are grouped under collapsible chapters when the course has them; your current section is marked "You are here", with the step you are on while a lesson is taught in steps, and a section the teacher has written ahead in the background shows "Lesson ready". A chapter counts a section as studied once its lesson is finished, which is also what fills the progress bar in the top bar. While the side panel is open beside the lesson, the route folds to a small capsule at the left, centred in the window, so the lesson keeps its width; point at it to open the full route beside it. Chapters are numbered badges whose rim fills as you study them, solid once finished and yellow for the chapter you are studying; select a badge in the full route to open or close its chapter. The capsule shows the sections of the chapter you are looking at under its badge. The arrow beside **Route** (or `r`) folds the route yourself when there is room, and the arrow at the top of the capsule unfolds it; the browser remembers your choice. Selecting another section previews it without moving your learning position; "Back to current section" returns. A later section's preview says how many sections come before it and offers **Study this section now**: after a confirmation naming the sections it will mark skipped (those before it without a finished lesson, the current one included) and any prerequisite you have not studied, it moves you there. Skipped sections stay on the route, as with a skipped chapter. A section shows "saved questions" when earlier questions are waiting for their full explanation there.
- **Chapters**: selecting a chapter opens its page: its sections with their progress, the chapter's open exercises, source coverage, the chapter recap, the chapter quiz and "Skip to the next chapter". The last section of a chapter ends with a link, "End of chapter: recap and quiz →", to this page. Once you have studied any section of the chapter, "Recap this chapter" writes a recap lesson on the page that connects the chapter's sections and lists exercises you set aside, without new material; it works from anywhere on the route and never moves your position, but waits while another lesson is being written. "Take the chapter quiz" asks a few of your earlier exercises from the chapter again (ones you solved with help or missed first, then ones you set aside, then ones you solved on your own, spread across the sections) together with two or three new questions spanning the chapter. Each earlier question is labelled with the section it came from. Answer from memory: each answer counts as a review of the sections it tests, and the quiz is optional, never a gate. The page shows how many questions you have answered and passed; once every question is answered you can take the quiz again, and earlier quizzes stay listed. Recaps and quiz questions are not counted among the last section's exercises. Skipped sections stay on the route, marked as skipped, and you can study them at any time; studying one clears the mark. A chapter that is not built yet offers "Build this chapter": Throughline builds it from your files in the background while you keep studying, and the route marks it "Building…" until the page fills in by itself. When you start the last built chapter, the next chapter is built the same way without asking. If a build fails, the page shows the error, "Try building again" and "Build this chapter in Codex".
- **Goal and next step**: the top of the current section shows its goal and, once a lesson has started, what to do next, following what has actually happened: follow the lesson while it is written, continue with the next step, try the open exercises, or move on. Before the first lesson the **Start this section** card says what comes next. **About this section** stays closed and shows the section's core question beside its title; open it for the steps, connections and sources. "Where you left off" appears when the course has a progress note.
- **Start lesson**: the first lesson of a chapter opens with an overview of the whole chapter; later sections open with a short bridge from the section before. Text streams in and is saved automatically when complete. "Ask for another pass" requests a new lesson in the same section.
- **Lesson steps**: a section's first lesson is taught in short steps, as many as the section needs, each marked "Step 2 of 3". Under a finished step, the rest of the plan is listed with **Continue**; a step the teacher added to fill a gap is marked "Added step". "Regenerate step" rewrites an unfinished step. A step may end with a **check question**: choose an option, or "I'm not sure", and its explanation appears at once, with the correct answer after a miss. A right answer moves on to the next step by itself; after a miss you press Continue when ready, and the next step opens by going over it. Check questions are never graded and do not count as practice. You can continue without answering, which closes the check. Lessons saved before steps still show as one reply.
- **Side questions**: select a sentence or formula in a lesson and choose "Ask about this passage", or open **Questions** in the top bar. The lesson keeps going unless you tick "Pause the lesson while I ask". Passages you asked about stay underlined in yellow, with a marker in the margin that opens the question. One box at the bottom of the panel asks a new question, or, while a thread is open, follows up on it: the line above the box says "Following up on" that question, and its × switches back to a new question. A passage selected in a lesson starts a new question; one selected in an answer follows up on that thread. In an open thread the question stays at the top of the panel while you read, and every question you asked, the first and each follow-up, reads the same way, with its quoted passage above it. "Back to the passage" sits beside the box while a thread is open. If the teacher suggests studying a foundation first, you decide. "Back to the passage" (or "Return to the lesson" for a question without a quoted passage) scrolls to what you asked about, even if the page has reflowed; it appears only while you are still in the section you asked from.
- **Many questions**: questions are listed oldest first, so a new one appears just above the box, each as a one-line summary (status, follow-ups, notes); select it or its arrow to open the thread. Only one thread is open at a time. A question opens by itself when you ask it or when it needs you (a foundation suggestion to decide, or an unfinished foundation supplement). The list can show this **Section**, this **Chapter** or **All**, each with a count, grouped under section and chapter headings; until you choose, it shows the narrowest that has questions. With more than four questions you can search question and answer text. Opening a question from a margin marker or saved explanation finds it whatever the filter.
- **Pending explanations**: when a question is answered briefly because a later section covers it properly, the thread says which section and links to it. That section lists the question under "Saved for this section", its lesson gives the full explanation, and the question is then marked "Explained".
- **Set aside for now**: the section overview lists source material the course plan deferred or flagged for review, with the reason, so nothing silently disappears from the route.
- **Sources**: hover over a citation to see the cited text. **Source** in the top bar shows or hides the reader. Click a citation to open the exact registered revision in the built-in reader: PDFs open at the cited page (outlined, with "Back to cited page" if you scroll away), with selectable text, zoom and fit-to-width; Markdown notes open at the highlighted line. Select text in the reader to ask about it. **All sources** in the reader header (or the Source tab before anything is open) lists every document in the course, those used by the section you are reading first, with their status and sections; choosing one opens it where this section uses it. "Not checked yet" stays visible until the location has been checked against the original, even when the model cites it. Drag the panel's left edge (or focus it and use the arrow keys) to resize it.
- **Highlights, notes and bookmarks**: select text in a lesson, a side-question answer, an exercise (its question or the teacher's feedback) or the source reader and choose **Highlight** or **Note**. Question threads and exercises with notes show a "1 note" button that opens them. Your highlights are green; asked passages are underlined in yellow. A pencil marker in the lesson margin opens the note. **Bookmark section** (next to the section number) and **Bookmark page** (in the source reader) mark places to come back to; bookmarked sections show a star in the route. **Notes** in the top bar (or `n`) lists everything for this **Section**, this **Chapter** or **All**, chosen and grouped the same way as questions, oldest first, and searchable once there are more than four; there you can edit, delete, jump back or export the shown notes as Markdown, and the box at the bottom adds a note to the section you are reading. These are yours alone: the teacher never sees them, and they do not count as progress. To get the teacher's view on something, ask a side question.
- **Practice**: exercises are prepared after the lesson's last step; until then the lesson's Continue is the way on. The top of Practice says how many exercises are solved and how many need another try. "Check my answer" asks the teacher to assess. You can type your answer or paste or attach images of handwritten work ("Attach image"), which the teacher reads with it. An exercise with several parts lists them ("(a)", or "Part 1" when the teacher gives no label) with a status for each once checked; answer them together or one at a time, and the exercise is solved once every part is correct. Hint, Key steps, Full solution and "Ask about this" are always available under "Stuck?", with "Do later" and **Skip** set apart; a solved exercise folds its answer box away, and "Answer again" opens it. After any help, or after feedback that corrects a part, a correct answer is recorded as solved with help, not on your own; feedback that only confirms correct parts is no help. "Ask about this" asks what the exercise or one of its parts means; the answer is never assessed. "Do later" keeps an exercise open; open exercises from earlier sections of the chapter follow you, under "Still open in this chapter". They stay with their chapter: in the next chapter they are on the earlier chapter's page, and moving on from a chapter's last section says so. **Skip** closes an exercise for good, with an optional reason (too easy, too tedious, not relevant); after two skips for the same reason the teacher suggests a matching preference. A homework part split from a larger question names that question and the sections that teach its other parts.
- **Enter next section** moves your position; practice is optional. Exercises not yet solved, including ones that need another try, stay open and follow you.
- **Review**: once you solve one of a section's exercises (on your own or with help), the section comes back for review a day later, then after 3, 7, 21 and 60 days. **Review** in the top bar shows how many sections are due today and opens the review page, oldest first and at most five a day; the rest wait for later days. "Start review" asks the teacher for one fresh question to answer from memory, with the usual Check my answer, Hint, Key steps and Full solution. Answering on your own moves the section to the next interval, a correct answer after help repeats the interval, and one that misses a part starts over; answering some parts correctly and leaving the rest for later counts as neither, and the review stays open. "Not now" puts a section off by one day. Review questions stay on the review page and are not counted among a section's exercises; sections skipped with their chapter are not reviewed until you study them. Reviews never block moving on.
- **Memory**: **Memory** in the top bar opens what the teacher remembers about you in this course: preferences, terms already taught and difficulties (ideas you have missed repeatedly, cleared once you get them right). The teacher uses them in every lesson; they are not a record of mastery, and your notes never enter them. Edit or delete any entry; a deleted entry is not added back. A preference is kept only once you confirm it: a **Remember: …?** banner (and the Memory panel) offers "Remember" or "Don't remember".
- **Changing the course**: the course is built and changed in Codex. Below the route, "Add files in Codex" (and "Check … source locations in Codex" when locations are not checked yet) opens a new Codex chat with a `$throughline` request typed in; an unchecked source location offers "Check this location in Codex". Each of these also copies the request, so with the Codex CLI you can paste it into Codex started in your Throughline folder. The page updates by itself when Codex is done.
- **Preparation**: while nothing else runs, the next section is prepared in the background; its status is shown in the "Up next" card. Prepared material is teacher-only and never counts as learning. Your own requests always take priority.

## Teacher settings

The teacher is the Codex CLI on this computer. In **Settings**, under Teacher, choose from the models your Codex sign-in offers: the teacher model (Codex default unless you pick one), the small model for preparing upcoming sections and consolidating notes (the teacher model unless you pick one), and a reasoning effort for each (the small model uses the teacher's unless you pick one). **Fast mode** appears when the teacher model offers a Fast tier; it speeds up lessons, answers and feedback at higher Codex usage, while background preparation keeps the standard speed. If Codex cannot list models, type a model name instead. `--model`, `--small-model`, `--effort` and `--small-effort` override the saved choice for one run.

## Keyboard shortcuts

`c` continue the lesson · `/` ask a side question · `[` and `]` previous and next section · `s` show or hide the source reader · `n` show or hide your notes · `m` show or hide course memory · `r` fold or unfold the route · `?` list the shortcuts · `Esc` close the route drawer, the side panel when it covers the lesson, or **Your courses** back to the open course.

## Pause, close and reopen

"Pause" stops generation and keeps the partial text as unfinished. "Regenerate" keeps the earlier attempt (under "Earlier unfinished attempts") and writes a complete new reply rather than continuing mid-sentence. If the model is still stopping, wait a moment and try again.

Closing the browser does not stop the host; work in progress continues. Stopping the host stops all model work. On reopen, the saved records return and any request cut off by the stop is shown as interrupted. Only one host can own a course at a time.

To move a course to another machine, stop the host and copy the whole course directory, including `course.sqlite3` and `sources/`.

## Troubleshooting

- **Port in use**: pass `--port`. If the course already has a host, use that window instead of starting a second one.
- **Model errors**: the lesson shows the message. Check that `codex` runs in the same terminal and that the model in Settings is available to your Codex account. Partial text is kept.
- **A PDF does not display**: use "Open original" in the reader header.
- **Preparation failed**: choose "Retry preparation"; learning continues without it.
- **"Reconnecting"** in the top bar: the browser lost the live connection. Your work is saved; it reconnects automatically once the host is reachable.
